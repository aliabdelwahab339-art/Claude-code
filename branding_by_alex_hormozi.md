# Branding by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Branding — How to Make Anyone or Anything Famous on Purpose)

## Core Idea
Branding is the deliberate pairing of your business with things your ideal customers like. Done right, it lowers your cost to acquire customers, raises how much they pay, and makes them loyal for life. A strong brand built on the right associations is the only lever that simultaneously improves all three business growth variables: more customers, higher value per customer, and longer retention.

## Key Frameworks

### What Branding Actually Is
**Branding = pairing two or more things together.**

- **Good branding** — pairing your business with something a majority of your ideal customers like → creates "towards" behavior (they seek you out)
- **Bad branding** — pairing your business with something they dislike → creates "away" behavior
- **No branding** — no memorable association → invisible; prospects treat you like any other unbranded option

The cattle branding analogy: a branded cow prompts a reaction (return it if you like the owner, ignore it if you don't). An unbranded cow gets no reaction. You want to be the branded cow.

**Branding vs. Advertising:**
- Advertising = letting people know about your stuff (reach)
- Branding = what they associate with you once they've been reached
- You can have great advertising + poor branding, or great branding + poor advertising

### Measuring Brand
Three variables:

1. **Reach** — how many people know about you (output of advertising). A brand can't exist without reach.
2. **Influence** — the probability your brand changes behavior when seen (strong brands move people; weak brands don't)
3. **Direction** — whether the behavior change is towards or away from you (relative to your target audience)

A "strong brand" = high reach × high influence × positive direction (for your target audience).

Politicians have extremely high influence (strong brands) even though some create "towards" and others create "away" reactions — both are strong brands, just for different audiences.

### Why Branding Makes Money
Three business levers branding improves:

1. **Lower CAC (more customers for less)**
   - Branded businesses convert higher percentages from advertising
   - Established brands guarantee sales on new products from day one
   - Example: Dwayne Johnson ("The Rock") launches any product → instant customers

2. **Raise LTV (make customers worth more)**
   - Strong brands charge 2x, 5x, or 10x more for the same thing
   - Example: Starbucks charges $5 for $1 coffee because of the brand experience

3. **Lower Risk (keep customers forever)**
   - Strong brands make customers loyal without needing to compete on price
   - Example: Once you're a "Mac" user, you typically only buy Apple for life

### Branded vs. Unbranded Ad Performance
- Unbranded ad ROI: ~4:1
- Branded ad ROI: ~45:1
- The same ad investment delivers dramatically more return once a brand is established

### The 4-Step Brand Building Process

**Step 1: Figure Out Who Your Ideal Customer Is**
- Be extremely specific — age, gender, income, aspirations, fears, daily life
- The more specific, the better you can identify what they like and dislike
- Vague customer definition = vague branding = no brand

**Step 2: Figure Out What They Like**
- What communities are they in? What celebrities do they admire?
- What brands do they already trust? What values do they hold?
- What style of communication resonates with them?
- You want to pair your brand with things they already have positive associations with

**Step 3: Associate Your Brand With Relevant Things They Like**
- Appear alongside things they love — events, people, aesthetics, values
- Co-create with people they trust — features, collaborations, endorsements
- Adopt language, references, and visual styles they recognize as "theirs"
- Every piece of content, every post, every visual choice either builds or erodes brand association

**Step 4: Optimize Your Associations for Max Profit**
- Track which associations are driving reach, influence, and direction
- Double down on what creates "towards" behavior in your target audience
- Eliminate associations that create "away" behavior, even if you personally like them
- Consider pivoting associations as your customer base evolves or as your brand matures

### The Supplement Company Lesson
Hormozi had the best product, the highest commissions (40% vs. 10-20%), the strongest formulations — and still couldn't get his friend to switch. Because the competitor had something intangible: **cool factor**. The brand was cool. His wasn't. Product alone loses to brand. You must invest in association, not just quality.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Define your ideal customer with extreme specificity before you build your brand
- Write down 10 things your ideal customer already likes, admires, and identifies with
- Associate your brand with those things deliberately — don't leave it to chance
- Track reach, influence, and direction as separate brand metrics
- Brand building doesn't mean logos and colors — it means curating associations
- The same ad investment delivers ~10x more return once a brand exists (45:1 vs 4:1)
- A product that's better in every measurable way can still lose to a brand that's "cooler"
- You only get one name — invest in it accordingly
- "Those who can't brand, do direct response. Those who can't do direct response, sell. Those who can't sell, stay poor."
- Branding is the only lever that simultaneously lowers CAC, raises LTV, and reduces churn
