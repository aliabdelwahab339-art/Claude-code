# Closing by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Closing — How to Turn Nos into Sales)

## Core Idea
Closing is the most important part of selling. It starts after you've asked the prospect to buy. Most businesses only capture leads from Bucket 1 (people who were going to buy anyway). The real money is in Bucket 3 — the Maybes — people who have reservations but could buy with the right conversation. Closing gives people the power to make a decision they're avoiding. Champions close.

## Key Frameworks

### The Three Advertising Buckets
Every ad splits your audience into three groups:
1. **YESes (Lay Downs)** — Value is so obvious they'll buy no matter what. The ad already closed them. Most businesses only capture these.
2. **NOs (Unqualified)** — Wrong person for the product. Bad targeting, or the ad attracted the wrong audience. Not worth closing.
3. **MAYBEs (On the Fence)** — They value what you sell but have reservations. This is the largest group and the biggest opportunity. This is who you train for.

Better advertising → more Maybes → bigger opportunity for closers to capture.

### Power: The Missing Ingredient
People on the fence avoid deciding because they lack decision-making power. Decision avoidance = neither yes nor no = revenue lost. Closing doesn't mean getting them to buy — it means giving them enough power to make a genuine decision either way.

Giving someone real decision-making power = you win either way:
- Best case: they buy and get the result
- Worst case: they decline, but you've done your job honestly
- Worst outcome: letting them stay on the "avoidance treadmill"

### The Onion of Blame
People with decision avoidance blame things in three layers (peel them back one at a time):

**Layer 1 — Circumstances (External)**
- Time: "I'm too busy" / "I don't have time right now"
- Money: "It's too expensive" / "I can't afford it"
- Fit: "It's not right for me" / "I need something different"

**Layer 2 — Other People**
- Authority: "I need to check with my partner/spouse first"
- Trust/Skepticism: "I've been burned before" / "Let me think about it" / "What makes you different?"

**Layer 3 — Self**
- Fear: "It might work for them but not for me"
- Avoidance: "I need to think about it" / "Send me a brochure"

Expect prospects to swap objections as you close. This is normal. Just keep peeling layers.

### The 24 Rules of Closing
1. When in doubt, repeat back what they said (active listening + buy time)
2. Acknowledge or agree, never disagree — disagreement creates hostility
3. Before getting real, ask permission ("Can I put my coach hat on for a second?")
4. Learn to stack closes — if one doesn't land, try from another angle
5. You don't need to memorize — understand the logic and use your own words
6. You only need 2-3 closes per main obstacle
7. Use nudges when someone is right on the edge
8. Practice on unqualified leads ("Yellows are golds") — volume builds skill
9. Volume negates luck — enough reps make world-class inevitable
10. Don't assume objections mean they won't buy — "It's expensive" is often just processing out loud
11. Never change your price on the spot to get a sale
12. If you feel like you're always in a battle, look at what happens *before* the call and at your pre-ask scripting
13. Only ask for the sale when you think you've got them
14. People want to believe you — help their brain justify the decision they already want to make
15. Selling happens before you ask for the sale. Closing happens after.
16. It's easier to handle problems before you ask for money than after
17. Expect and plan for no — it's not failure, it's expected
18. Price shock isn't a "no" — unless they say there's a problem, assume they're processing
19. Selling properly is the first step to becoming a coach
20. **The person who cares most about the prospect wins the sale** — keep their long-term wellbeing at the center
21. Seek to understand, not to argue
22. Selling is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust
23. Belief and trust are a continuum, not binaries — you can always improve
24. Closers ask hard questions because they genuinely care

### All-Purpose Closes (by Objection Category)

**When They Blame Circumstances:**
- **Time:** Reframe the time cost of inaction vs. action. "What's the cost of not solving this in 6 months?"
- **Money:** Reframe the investment vs. the cost of not buying. Connect current pain to future outcome.
- **Fit:** Probe deeper on what "fit" means to them; often a cover for another objection

**When They Blame Other People:**
- **Decision Makers:** "Tell me about a past time [partner] supported you in a big decision like this."
- **Been Burned Before:** "What would need to be true for you to feel safe moving forward?" Then address it.

**When They Blame Themselves:**
- **Fear:** "What's the worst that could realistically happen if it doesn't work the way you want?"
- **Avoidance:** Hard question + genuine care: "What's really holding you back?"

## Actionable Takeaways
- Stop targeting only "lay downs" — the money is in the Maybes
- Memorize the Onion of Blame: Circumstances → Other People → Self. Every objection fits one layer.
- Expect multiple objections from each prospect — it's normal, not failure
- Never disagree; acknowledge and reframe
- The person who cares most about the prospect wins the sale — detach from needing the commission
- Only need 2-3 closes per main obstacle type — depth beats breadth
- Practice on unqualified leads — volume of reps is the fastest path to world class
- "Volume negates luck" — if you do enough sales calls, skill becomes inevitable
- Train your sales team on closing daily, not once — it was daily drilling that took Hormozi from 0% to $1.5M/mo
- Selling is transference of belief over a bridge of trust — build genuine belief in your product first
