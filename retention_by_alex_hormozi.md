# Retention by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Retention — How to Get Customers to Keep Buying)

## Core Idea
Retention is not a "nice to have" — it is the foundation of a sustainable business. A business with high churn is a leaky bucket: you're constantly refilling what leaks out. Reducing churn from 10% to 3% is a 3.3x increase in lifetime value with zero additional ad spend. The Five Horsemen of Retention, discovered by interviewing gym owners with the lowest churn rates, can transform a revolving-door business into one with compounding value.

## Key Frameworks

### What Is Churn?
Churn = the percentage of customers who leave over a specific time period.

Formula:
- Last Period Customers: 100
- This Period Customers: 95
- Churn = (100 - 95) / 100 = 5%

Key rule: New customers added during the period don't reduce churn. If 5 of the original 100 left, churn is 5% — regardless of how many new customers you added.

### Why Churn Destroys Businesses
At 15% monthly churn, a gym with 250 members on January 1st needs 208 *new* members by December 31st just to stay flat. Missing by 2 members/month = business death.

**The Churn Math:**
- 10% churn → Average customer lifespan: 10 months
- 3% churn → Average customer lifespan: 33 months
- **Reducing churn from 10% to 3% = 3.3x increase in LTV**

### Price, Value, and Churn
Warren Buffett: "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."

Customers stay when perceived value exceeds price paid. To improve retention:
1. Increase value delivered (preferred)
2. Lower price (last resort)

**Invert the question:** Instead of "How do I retain customers?", ask: "What would cause 100% churn?"

Causes of maximum churn:
- Ignore them
- Break promises
- Miscommunicate
- Treat them poorly
- Set unrealistic expectations
- Hide progress and updates
- Keep them away from other happy customers
- Make the product harder to use

**Retention playbook = do the exact opposite of all the above.**

### The Five Horsemen of Retention
Discovered through common-factors analysis of gym owners with <3% monthly churn:

**1. Track Attendance**
- Members who visit 3x/week stay; members who visit ≤2x/week eventually cancel
- Churn pattern: 3 sessions → 2 sessions → 2 sessions → 1 session → CANCEL
- **The rescue window:** When attendance drops to 2 sessions, reach out immediately — you can recover them
- Intervention turns: 3 → 2 (reach out) → 3 → 3 → 3 (retained)

**2. Reach Out 2x Per Week**
- Proactive outreach praising participation and progress dramatically improves retention
- Solving small problems before they become cancellation reasons prevents churn
- Both praise AND problem-solving are important — do both

**3. Handwritten Cards**
- Send at onboarding, at referral asks (3, 6, 12 months), and at holidays/birthdays
- Handwritten = perceived as personal, memorable, and rare
- "There's never a bad time to send a handwritten card so long as you make it yourself"

**4. Member Events**
- Hold events on a regular internal cadence (every 21, 42, or 63 days) but make them feel random to members
- Use handwritten invites
- Events simultaneously reduce churn AND generate referrals — a compounding win

**5. Exit Interviews**
- Set the expectation at onboarding that cancellations require an exit interview
- When a cancellation notice comes in: use it as the trigger to schedule the exit interview
- Done right: saves ~50% of phone/email cancellations
- Realistic result: reduces churn by 25% → 33% increase in LTV

### The Five Horsemen in Action
Implementation results for gym owners:
- Month 1: Churn UP by 50% ("shaking the tree" — billing zombies and near-churners surface)
- Month 2: Churn DOWN by 50%
- Month 3: Churn DOWN by another 50%
- Final state: 10% churn → 7% → 3% (over 3 months)

The initial spike is expected and temporary — it's existing churners surfacing, not new ones being created.

### Skool Data (Additional Retention Signals)
From Skool (Hormozi's membership platform with millions of members), hard data confirms additional churn reducers:
- Community engagement (members connected to other members churn less)
- Progress visibility (showing members their own advancement reduces cancel temptation)
- Regular value delivery (consistent new content or sessions keeps perceived value high)

## Actionable Takeaways
- Know your monthly churn rate — it is one of the most important numbers in your business
- Reducing churn from 10% to 3% = 3.3x LTV — no advertising needed
- Track attendance/usage — declining engagement is the leading indicator of churn before it happens
- Reach out to customers 2x/week — proactively, not just when there's a problem
- Send handwritten cards at onboarding, milestones, and referral asks
- Hold member events every 21-42 days to build community and generate referrals
- Set the expectation of exit interviews at onboarding — not at the point of cancellation
- Expect churn to spike in Month 1 when you implement the Five Horsemen — it's "shaking the tree," not a failure
- "I have yet to see a business with less than 3% churn that does not make great money."
- A leaky bucket business is barely a business — fix retention before scaling acquisition
