# Lead Nurture by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Lead Nurture)

## Core Idea
Lead nurture is about maximizing 30-day show rates — getting engaged leads to schedule and actually show up to buy. Most businesses ignore show rates because they think they can't do anything about them. They're wrong. The Four Pillars of Lead Nurture (Availability, Speed, Personalization, Volume) can increase revenue 20-40% with zero additional ad spend — by simply capturing customers you already paid for.

## Key Frameworks

### The Three Key Metrics
- **Schedule Rate** — percentage of engaged leads who schedule an appointment
- **Show Rate** — percentage of scheduled leads that actually show up
- **Throughput** — the total percentage of engaged leads who show up (Schedule Rate × Show Rate)

Example: 100 leads → 50% schedule = 50 booked → 50% show = 25 shown → 25% throughput

A 20-40% increase in show rates can translate to a 2x-3x increase in profit (at 20% margins).

### The Four Pillars of Lead Nurture
Discovered from analyzing 1 million+ appointment rows of data:

**Pillar I: Availability**
- The more open appointment slots you have, the higher the show rate
- More days, more hours, more slots = more people who can schedule when they're ready
- Most businesses lose leads simply because no appointment slot is available when the lead has momentum
- Fix: Expand availability; accept walk-ins where possible; use online scheduling that shows real-time openings

**Pillar II: Speed**
- The faster you contact a lead after they opt in, the higher the show rate
- Contact within 60 seconds → **391% increase** in conversion vs. contact after 5+ minutes
- Average competitor response time: 42 hours (real InsideSales.com data)
- Limit how far out leads can schedule — the further out the appointment, the lower the show rate
- Best practice: Limit scheduling to 3 days out; contact leads within 60 seconds
- Fix: Use automation for immediate response; have staffed coverage during high-lead hours

**Pillar III: Personalization**
- The more personal the follow-up, the more likely leads respond
- The more leads respond to follow-ups, the more likely they show up
- Templated, generic messages get ignored; personalized messages get replies
- Personalization = using information the lead already gave you (name, problem, location, reason for inquiry)
- Fix: Reference specific details in every touchpoint; segment follow-up sequences by lead type/source

**Pillar IV: Volume**
- The more follow-up attempts before giving up, the higher the show rate
- Average business: 1.3 attempts before giving up; 44% of salespeople stop after just 1 attempt (RAIN Group)
- Best businesses: 5+ attempts across multiple channels before disqualifying
- Fix: Build 5-8 touchpoint sequences using email + text + call combinations

### The Ideal Follow-Up Sequence
Combine all four pillars:
1. Immediate contact within 60 seconds of opt-in (Speed)
2. Multiple appointment slots shown to the lead (Availability)
3. Personalized messaging using their submitted information (Personalization)
4. 5+ follow-ups across multiple channels before disqualifying (Volume)

### The Throughput Math
Improving show rates from 25% to 50% doesn't double revenue — it may 3-4x profit because the marginal cost of serving the extra shows is near zero. All the advertising cost is already sunk.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Measure your current schedule rate, show rate, and throughput — this is your baseline
- Contact every new lead within 60 seconds — even an automated "we got your info" message matters
- Limit scheduling to 3 days out maximum — the further out, the more likely they no-show
- Expand your available appointment slots — more slots = more schedule opportunities
- Build a 5+ touch follow-up sequence: Call → Text → Email → Call → Email
- Personalize every message with the lead's name and specific reason for inquiry
- 44% of salespeople give up after one attempt — being the one who follows up 5+ times is a competitive moat
- A 20-40% increase in show rates can 2-3x profit with zero additional ad spend
- "If they do not show, they cannot buy."
