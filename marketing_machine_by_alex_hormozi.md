# Marketing Machine by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Marketing Machine — How to Turn Your Business Into an Ads Machine)

## Core Idea
A Marketing Machine is a system that turns your existing business activities into a continuous stream of ads — without you being in them. 80% of Hormozi's top 50 ads at Gym Launch had no his face in them. They were customer-generated. Any business can implement this because it runs on what you're already doing. The result: more ads, better ads, and a business that doesn't depend on any one person's face — which also dramatically increases enterprise value.

## Key Frameworks

### The Core Problem: Keyman Risk
A business where the founder's face is required for advertising is unsellable. If the key person leaves, gets sick, or wants out — the business shrinks. Every investor discounts for this.

The Marketing Machine solves keyman risk in advertising by engineering a system where customers generate the ads instead of the founder. The end result:
- More ads (customers are producing constantly)
- Better ads (customer stories outperform founder-direct ads)
- No keyman risk (the machine runs without you)

### The Core Insight
"The bottom of the list feeds the top of the list."

Every business already has interactions with customers — sales calls, onboarding, results, events, support, upsells, testimonials. These interactions, when captured systematically, produce advertising material automatically. You don't generate ads. You capture them from activities you're already doing.

### The 7 Nodes of the Marketing Machine

Each node is a reliable source of customer-generated content:

**☐ 1. Lifecycle**
- Capture moments throughout the customer journey: first result, milestone achieved, renewal, upgrade
- Script these captures into your delivery process — don't wait for them to come to you
- Examples: first win email ask, milestone video request, post-result survey with video option

**☐ 2. Social Media**
- Monitor and collect what customers already post about you on their own platforms
- Set up alerts, dedicated hashtags, or community tags
- Ask customers to tag you when they share wins
- Repost, use in ads (with permission), incorporate in testimonial libraries

**☐ 3. In-Person Events**
- Every event = an ad production opportunity
- Record award ceremonies, transformations, group energy, testimonials
- Give prizes for best "ad-quality" videos produced at events
- Events are the highest emotional intensity moments → highest quality testimonials

**☐ 4. Communication, Communities & Review Sites**
- Scrape your own CRM, email threads, community chats for positive language
- Monitor review sites (Google, Trustpilot, etc.) for material
- Ask happy customers to formalize their casual praise into video testimonials
- Identify natural promoters who are already talking about you; activate them

**☐ 5. Bonuses, Milestones & Awards**
- Award systems create photo/video opportunities that double as ad content
- Milestone recognition (30 days in, 90 days, 1 year) generates retrospective testimonials
- Create a leaderboard program → top performers create braggable content
- Their celebration = your advertising

**☐ 6. Competition**
- Internal competitions among customers (challenges, leaderboards, contests)
- Competitors post about their progress and results = ad-ready content
- The competition mechanic creates social proof at scale — community achievements become visible

**☐ 7. Delivery**
- Capture testimonials at every delivery touchpoint: onboarding calls, coaching calls, support calls, upsell calls
- Train delivery team to recognize and capture "mic drop" moments
- End every call with: "Can I ask a quick favor — would you be willing to record a 30-second video about your experience?"

### Implementation Principle: One > None
Don't get overwhelmed by all 7 nodes. Pick the easiest one to implement for your business and start there. One system in place is infinitely better than seven planned and zero running. Build sequentially.

### Why Customer Ads Outperform Founder Ads
At Gym Launch, when Hormozi reviewed thousands of ads years later:
- **80% of top 50 performers had no Hormozi in them**
- Customer faces are more relatable to the target audience than the founder's face
- Prospects see themselves in customer testimonials — they see aspirations, not authority
- Customer stories are perceived as more authentic and credible than any founder pitch

### Enterprise Value Impact
A business whose advertising depends on the founder = high keyman risk = discount on sale price.
A business whose advertising runs on a customer-powered machine = low keyman risk = premium on sale price.

This distinction was worth millions to Hormozi in the Gym Launch sale ($46.2M).
