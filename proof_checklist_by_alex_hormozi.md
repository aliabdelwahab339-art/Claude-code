# Proof Checklist by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Proof Checklist — How to Get Strangers to Believe You Instantly)

## Core Idea
80% of Hormozi's top-performing ads told one story: "This. Product. Works." They had no educational content, no offer-focus, and no Hormozi center-stage. The single most common difference between top performers and bottom performers was **proof**. Everyone can copy an advertising claim, but no one can produce your exact results to back it up. Your promise is not a differentiator. Your proof is.

## Key Frameworks

### What Proof Is
**Proof = data that supports your advertising claims.**

An advertising claim is anything you say has happened, might happen, or will happen about what you sell. The most compelling claims indicate superior results on faster timelines with modest effort. The more compelling your claim, the more proof you need to back it up.

**Belief is a continuum, not binary.** The question isn't "do they believe or not?" — it's "how much do they believe?" Every piece of proof moves prospects along this continuum toward absolute certainty.

**Pro Tip:** Most marketers find proof to support their claims. Instead, collect your data first, then make claims the data already supports. Don't claim and then prove — claim your proof. If your proof isn't compelling, fix reality first (give free services, keep improving your product until the truth is compelling).

### The 13 Proof Hierarchy Rules

Each rule is a spectrum — the left side is always more compelling than the right side:

**1. In-Person > Virtual**
Seeing something with their own two eyes beats seeing it on screen. A live pavement-cleaning demo at someone's door > the same demo on a Zoom call.
- *Make it happen:* Fly your best customers to big events to tell their stories live. The sales lift from in-person proof more than covers the flight costs.

**2. Live > Recorded**
A live testimonial on stage or webinar > a pre-recorded video of the same testimonial.
- *Make it happen:* Give customers examples of great testimonials; incentivize them to appear live.

**3. Raw > Processed**
An unedited iPhone video testimonial > a highly polished, professionally edited version of the same testimonial. Minimum tampering = maximum authenticity.
- *Make it happen:* Give customers a simple structure, then incentivize them to send raw videos. Build testimonial prompting into your customer journey.

**4. Show > Tell**
A before-and-after video showing hair transformation > describing the hair transformation verbally. An unboxing video showing exactly what they'll receive > telling them what they'll receive.
- *Make it happen:* Record client experiences, especially at breakthrough moments. Ask customers to document key results. For test prep, get students to film their reaction to seeing their scores. Incentivize documentation if needed.

**5. Other People Saying Your Stuff's Great > You Saying Your Stuff's Great**
No one cares what you think about your own product. You are the most biased person who exists. Customer reviews outperform any company-generated content.
- *Make it happen:* Build a review-request step into your customer journey. Put them on Google, Trustpilot, G2, or wherever your ideal customers look.

**6. Identical to Them > Opposite of Them**
Testimonials from people who look like the prospect are dramatically more persuasive. When Hormozi ran ads featuring female gym owners, he got more female gym owners. When he ran ads featuring older owners, he got more older owners.
- *Make it happen:* Identify your demographic segments (age, gender, ethnicity, industry). Ensure your proof library reflects all of them. When you have multiple customer avatars, show results for each.

**7. Personal > Generic**
"I was a 52-year-old gym owner in Ohio losing $3,000/month and within 30 days I had 87 new members" > "People are getting great results." The more specific, the more believable.
- *Make it happen:* When gathering testimonials, ask for specifics: before state, after state, timeframe, key numbers.

**8. Big Results > Small Results > No Results**
A prospect who lost 50 lbs is more compelling than one who lost 5 lbs. Both are more compelling than no results shown. Lead with your best results.
- *Make it happen:* Actively identify your "hero customers" — the ones with the most impressive outcomes — and capture their stories first.

**9. Newer Proof > Older Proof**
Recent results are more credible than old ones. "Last month" > "three years ago."
- *Make it happen:* Maintain an ongoing system for capturing new testimonials. Never let your proof library go stale. Rotate newer proof to the front.

**10. More Proof > Less Proof**
Overwhelming proof beats a single testimonial. This is why volume of testimonials matters, not just quality.
- *Make it happen:* Build proof collection into every stage of your customer journey. The goal is a library, not a handful of examples.

**11. Third Party Verification > Zero Verification**
A screenshot of a real bank statement > you saying "my clients make a lot of money." Third-party verification (certifications, government data, independent audits, review platforms) dramatically increases believability.
- *Make it happen:* Always push customers to verify results with hard data. Screenshots, certificates, report cards, platform screenshots — anything independently verifiable.

**12. Proof With Numbers > Proof Without Numbers**
"I went from $32,000/month to $127,000/month in 90 days" > "My income skyrocketed."
Numbers create specificity and credibility simultaneously.
- *Make it happen:* Always ask customers for the specific before/after numbers. Revenue, weight, time saved, followers gained, etc.

**13. Metaphors > Technical Jargon**
Simple analogies that connect to prospect experience > complex technical explanations of mechanism.
"We put your marketing on autopilot" > "We leverage AI-powered behavioral trigger sequences."
- *Make it happen:* Translate every technical claim into a vivid, relatable analogy before presenting it to prospects.

### The Discovery Story
Hormozi analyzed 5 years of Gym Launch ads when a fund manager's call revealed ad costs had doubled. Sorting by performance and bucketing by theme, he found that the top performers shared one consistent attribute: overwhelming proof. The thousands of low-performing ads were about the business, the offer, the mechanism — not about customers winning. The lesson: **don't talk about you; show them.**

### Match Claims to Proof
The order of operations most businesses use:
1. Make a claim → try to find proof to back it up (hard, and often misleading)

The better order:
1. Collect real results → make claims your results already support (easy, credible, unique)

"If your proof isn't compelling — fix reality." Give away free services. Work until the truth is your best marketing.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Analyze your best-performing ads vs. current ads — what element separates them?
- Build a dedicated proof library sorted by: medium (video/text/image), result size, and customer avatar
- Integrate testimonial collection into every stage of your customer journey
- Lead with numbers: specific before/after states with timeframes
- Feature proof from prospects who look exactly like your ideal buyer
- Use raw iPhone videos over polished productions — authenticity > aesthetics
- Ask customers to submit live testimonials at events — they outperform every other format
- Match your proof to your claim — better yet, let your proof dictate your claims
- Your promise is not a differentiator. Your proof is.
- "Everyone can make the same promise, but no one can have the same proof."
