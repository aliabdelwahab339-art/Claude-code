## Core Idea
Free and discount promotions are "wrappers" around your Grand Slam Offer — they make your core offer more attractive to cold audiences without changing what's inside. Hormozi's entry strategy into every new market: start with something free or heavily discounted to generate flow, prove results, collect testimonials, and build conviction. Then add friction and monetize more aggressively. "Get Flow. Monetize Flow. Then Add Friction."

## Key Frameworks

### The Three Promotion Types
Every Grand Slam Offer can be presented in three ways, from cold to warm audiences:

1. **Premium Promotions** — Present the offer at full price with no wrapper. Best for warm audiences with established trust.
2. **Free Promotions** — Give the core offer (or an entry-level version) away for free. Best for generating maximum lead volume and initial traction.
3. **Discount Promotions** — Deeply discount the offer to lower the barrier to entry. Middle ground between premium and free.

**Important:** Free and discount are wrappers around the core Grand Slam Offer. They enhance attraction without changing what's inside.

### Why Hormozi Starts with Free or Discounted Offers
Four reasons:
1. **Learning margin** — In a new market, you don't know what you're doing yet. Free gives forgiveness if it's rough.
2. **Testimonials first** — Working free gets you testimonials fast. Testimonials fuel future paid sales.
3. **Conviction building** — Offering for free helps you close the first sales even when you lack certainty in your own pitch.
4. **Referral engine** — Starting free makes referrals and demand generation easy.

**Example:** Hormozi did Gym Launch as a pure free offer for 18 months — he flew to gyms, ran ads with his own money, closed the clients, gave them all the customers, and only kept the revenue he generated. Made his first $1M from a free offer.

### The Premium Offer

**When to use it:** You have an established reputation, proven results, and a warm audience.

**The math of premium offers:**
- Business A: $100/sale ($50 profit) → needs 190 sales to make $9,500 profit
- Business B: $10,000/sale ($9,500 profit) → needs 1 sale to make $9,500 profit
- If you offer the $10,000 offer to those same 190 leads, close ~5% → 9.5 sales × $9,500 = **$90,250** (9.5x more profitable)

**Pros:**
- Simplest math (one thing to sell)
- Highest quality customers — premium price filters for motivated buyers
- Transparent selling environment — prospect expects to be pitched
- Highest margin per transaction

**Cons:**
- Most expensive cost per lead
- Requires excellent copy and deep avatar understanding
- Requires polished sales process
- Least efficient at capturing marketplace volume

**Bottom line:** Premium alone doesn't work well for beginners. Start with free/discount to prove results and build conviction, then transition to premium.

### The Free Offer

**The "Penny Gap" effect (Dr. Dan Ariely):** 9x more people take a free Hershey kiss than one sold for a penny. Free isn't just cheap — it's a categorically different psychological trigger.

**When a free offer doesn't work:**
1. Prospect doesn't want what you're giving away (change what you offer or how you describe it)
2. Prospect doesn't believe the offer (even "I'll give you $1,000 for every $100 you give me" wouldn't convert without believability)
3. Wrong audience — targeting mismatch (e.g., single ladies promo to married moms)

**Free Offer Pros:**
- Highest lead volume (most people raise their hand)
- Lowest cost per lead
- Works for any business stage
- Creates viral and referral potential

**Free Offer Cons:**
- Can attract unqualified buyers ("freebie seekers")
- May create operational overload if volume is too high
- Requires careful design to ensure the follow-on paid offer is naturally positioned

**Managing volume through friction:**
Friction increases lead quality while reducing quantity. Types:
1. **Increased qualifications** — "You must be over 25, employed, and a homeowner"
2. **Application forms** — More questions = more friction = higher quality
3. **Multi-step processes** — More steps = fewer completions = higher intent
4. **Forced consumption** — Make prospects watch a 40-minute video before seeing a CTA
5. **Long-form ads** — A 2-hour video before the CTA selects for committed prospects

**Free Offer Math:**
Even when 50% of free leads are unqualified, the total economics often win over a more selective paid offer — because the volume advantage outweighs the qualification loss. Do the math before assuming higher friction = better results.

### The Discount Offer
The middle ground between free and premium. Reduces friction vs. premium, but requires some commitment vs. free. Used to:
- Bridge new customers who trust you somewhat but not fully
- Re-engage warm leads who need a push
- Build initial momentum in a market with some existing awareness

### The Framework for Entering a New Market
1. Start with a free offer (max lead volume, fast testimonials)
2. Add friction over time as you prove your process
3. Transition to discount offers as you build trust and proof
4. Graduate to premium offers with an established audience
5. Throughout: keep one low/free front-end offer to maintain lead flow

## Actionable Takeaways
- When entering a new market, start with a free or heavily discounted offer — not premium
- Use free offers to generate testimonials, build conviction, and learn what your customers want
- "Get Flow. Monetize Flow. Then Add Friction." — in that order, not reverse
- The penny gap is real: free converts 9x better than even $0.01
- Premium is 9.5x more profitable per transaction — but only works when trust exists
- Use friction (qualifications, forms, video requirements) to improve free lead quality without raising price
- Proof from free work is what makes premium offers sell later — do the free work first
- Don't start with what you're passionate about — start with what your customers want next
- Free and discount are wrappers — the Grand Slam Offer inside stays the same
- "Those who give the most, get the most."
