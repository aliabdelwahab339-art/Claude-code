# GOATed Ads by Alex Hormozi

## Core Idea
Ad fatigue is not a market saturation problem — it's an ad quality problem. The businesses that scale past $5K–$10K/day in ad spend are the ones making 500+ ads per month instead of 5. By breaking ads into three modular parts (Hook, Meat, CTA) and using an "Ad Kaleidoscope" to multiply variations, you can make 150–750 ads per week and never hit a ceiling.

## Key Frameworks

### The Three-Part Ad Structure
Every ad is made of three interchangeable parts:
1. **Hook** — First 3–5 seconds. Gets attention and filters for the right audience.
2. **Meat** — The body. Delivers the story, proof, or argument.
3. **CTA** — Call to Action. Tells them exactly what to do next.

These are modular: 50 hooks × 3 meats × 3 CTAs = 450 unique ads.

### The Ad Assembly Process
Stop "creating" ads and start "assembling" them:
- **80% of prep time → Hooks** (if they don't watch the hook, nothing else matters)
- **20% of prep time → Meat** (3–5 well-scripted body versions)
- **~0% → CTAs** (simple, reusable, interchangeable)

Prep takes 2–3 hours. Recording takes 1–2 hours. Result: 150–750 ads per session.

### The Ad Kaleidoscope
Take your best-performing ad and systematically vary each component:
- Vary the hook (50 different opening lines)
- Vary the framing (story, testimonial, direct, educational)
- Vary the presenter (different people or styles)
- Vary the CTA (different next steps)
This prevents competitors from copying your top performers and provides endless creative fuel.

### The Awareness Pyramid (Eugene Schwartz Adaptation)
Audiences exist on a spectrum from cold to warm:
1. **Most Aware** — knows your product, just needs the deal
2. **Product-Aware** — knows what you sell but isn't sure it's right for them
3. **Solution-Aware** — knows the result they want, doesn't know your product
4. **Problem-Aware** — knows they have a problem, doesn't know solutions exist
5. **Completely Unaware** — doesn't know they have a problem

As you scale ad spend, you move up the pyramid to colder audiences. Better ads convert colder audiences. This is why volume of creative directly correlates with how much you can spend.

### Finding High-Converting Hooks
Four sources for winning hooks:
1. **Previous winning ads** — reuse what already worked
2. **High-performing free content** — steal hooks from your best posts/videos
3. **Other people's ads** — collect ads you see in your feed, note what stops you
4. **Experimental hooks** — test new angles to expand into new audience segments

### Scale It: The Volume Playbook
- Most businesses make 5 ads/month → saturate quickly
- Target: 150+ ads/week (after assembly process)
- Devote one dedicated day per week to recording (e.g., "Ad Friday")
- As creative volume increases, algorithm has more to test → winning ads found faster

## Chapter Summaries

**GOATed Ads** — Origin story: a portfolio company spending $200K/month on ads hitting a wall. Diagnosis: 5 ads/month is not enough. Solution: switch to an assembly model making 500+ per month. Company doubled in two quarters.

**Why Ads Hit a Wall** — The Awareness Pyramid explains why great ads that convert warm audiences can't convert cold audiences. You need better (and more) creative, not a new market.

**The Ad Assembly Process** — Break ad creation into chunks: hooks first (80% of time), then meat (20%), then CTA (minimal time). Record in batches, not one at a time.

**The Ad Meat** — Types of content for the body: story-based, proof-based, educational, direct offer. Each type works better at different audience awareness levels.

**CTA — Call to Action** — Simple, direct, single action. Don't confuse the audience. Match the CTA to the audience temperature (cold → content; warm → call/buy).

**Scale It** — The full weekly ad production system. One day per week dedicated to ad creation produces 600+ ads/month. Volume is a competitive moat.

**GOATed Ads Cheat Sheet** — Summary of the full process on one page.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Break every ad into Hook, Meat, CTA — these are separate, interchangeable modules
- Spend 80% of ad prep time on hooks — nothing else matters if they don't watch the first 5 seconds
- Set a goal of 50+ new hooks per recording session
- Dedicate one fixed day per week to recording new ads
- If your ads are plateauing, the problem is creative volume, not market saturation
- Use the Ad Kaleidoscope: vary one element at a time across 50 combinations
- Mine your best free content for hooks — use what already proved it can stop a scroll
- Keep a "swipe file" of ads that stopped you while scrolling — reverse-engineer their hooks
- 150–750 ads/week is the target; most businesses are at 5–20
- The best defense against competitors copying your ads is volume — they can't copy all 600
