# Hooks by Alex Hormozi
## ($100M Playbook: Hooks That Get Clicks)

## Core Idea
A hook is whatever gets the attention of your audience first — the opening seconds of a video, the first image, the first line of an email. If nobody notices your ad, nothing else matters. Mastering hooks can 5x, 10x, or even 100x the number of people who pay attention to your ads and content — changing nothing else. Spend 80% of your advertising prep time on hooks.

## Key Frameworks

### What a Hook Is
A hook has two parts:
1. **The Call Out** — gets the prospect to think "this is for me." Works via the "cocktail party effect" — even in a noisy feed, the right signal cuts through and demands attention.
2. **The Condition for Value** — a promise that consuming this thing will deliver value. Sometimes explicit ("If you're a mom, watch this to learn how to get your kids to behave"), sometimes implicit (showing a result and saying "look how I did it").

### Types of Hooks

**Verbal Hooks:**
- **Labels** — "Local business owners, I have a gift for you" (words the avatar identifies with)
- **Yes Questions** — "Would you pay $1,000 to have the business of your dreams in 30 days?"
- **Open Questions** — "Which would you rather be?"
- **Conditionals** — "If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong sh*t."
- **Commands** — "Read this if you're tired of being broke."
- **Statements** — "The smartest thing you can do today…" / "How to get ahead of 99% of people."
- **Lists or Steps** — "In this video I'm going to talk to you about the 28 ways to stay poor."
- **Narratives** — "One day I was in the back and this old lady comes in and she was piss angry."
- **Exclamations** — "Ahhhhh… This is the blueprint to becoming a millionaire."

**Nonverbal Hooks:**
- Unexpected visuals, movement, props (e.g., catching a banana)
- Sounds that trigger the cocktail party effect
- Setting, expressions, or anything that visually signals: "something interesting is happening here"

### The 70-20-10 Hook Innovation Rule
Borrowed from Google's resource allocation model:
- **70% (Core)** — Use proven hooks from your own best-performing past content. Create a spreadsheet tracking: Name, Hook, Views, Link — per platform. Always review before recording.
- **20% (Emerging)** — Model hooks from "Winner Adjacent" — things working in adjacent niches. Subscribe to outlier-tracking tools or search top content by topic.
- **10% (Experimental)** — Try entirely new formats and angles. If it works, it graduates to 70%. If it bombs, document it and don't repeat.

### Testing and Recording Protocol
- For **ads**: Record 10 hooks for every 1 piece of ad content (10x ratio)
- For **short content**: Turn each winning tweet/post into a short, then expand on the hook
- For **long content**: Record 3-4 hook variations; let post-production pick the best
- For **emails**: Run active A/B split tests on every campaign; document winners in a separate file

### Let the Data Do the Teaching
If you're starting from zero, just make a bunch and see what happens. Then:
- Look at the top 10% of your content — examine the hooks
- Look at the bottom 10% — make less of that
- Repeat indefinitely — the data teaches you better than any coach

## Chapter Summaries

**The Dean Graziosi Story** — The origin: an infomercial with Larry King bombed. One sentence change to the hook — shifting from "here's who Dean is" to "have you ever looked in the mirror and thought you should be further ahead by now?" — turned it into a million-book-selling monster. The entire business value was in the first two sentences.

**What's a Hook?** — Definition and the two-part structure: call out + condition for value. The cocktail party effect. Why spending 8 of 10 hours on hooks is rational.

**Types of Hooks** — Verbal (labels, questions, commands, statements, narratives) and nonverbal (visual, sound). Best hooks combine both.

**The Process** — 70-20-10 framework for allocating hook creation effort. Track performance by platform. Let data teach.

**Best Performing Hooks** — 121 actual top hooks across Ads, YouTube, Instagram, Leila Hormozi YouTube/Instagram, Email, and Twitter/X.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Spend 8 of every 10 hours of ad prep time on hooks alone
- For every 1 piece of ad content, record 10 different hook variations
- Build a hook tracker spreadsheet with Name, Hook, Views, and Link — one tab per platform
- Review your top-performing hooks before every recording session
- Use the 70-20-10 model: 70% proven, 20% adjacent winners, 10% new experiments
- If you're starting from scratch, just produce — then let the data tell you what worked
- Study the hooks that stop you while scrolling — reverse-engineer them
- Nonverbal hooks (unexpected visuals, sounds, props) can stop a scroll without a single word
- Changing only the hook — nothing else — can turn a bombing ad into a monster
