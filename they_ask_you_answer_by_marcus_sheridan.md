# They Ask, You Answer by Marcus Sheridan

## Core Idea
The internet has permanently shifted buying decisions — today, 70% of a buying decision is made before a prospect ever contacts a company. The businesses that win are the ones that become the most trusted teachers in their industry by obsessively answering every question their customers ask, even the uncomfortable ones about price, problems, and competitors.

## Key Frameworks

### They Ask, You Answer Philosophy
Simple rule: if a customer has ever asked you a question — in person, by email, on the phone — you should answer it in writing, video, or content on your website. Every question is a content opportunity. No question is too basic or too sensitive.

### The Big 5 Content Subjects
The five topics that drive the most buying decisions and organic traffic:

1. **Pricing & Costs** — The #1 question customers have and the #1 thing businesses refuse to answer. Talking about price openly builds trust and pre-qualifies buyers. Sheridan's pool company generated $3M+ in sales from one article about pool pricing.
2. **Problems** — Address your product's weaknesses honestly. Counterintuitively, acknowledging flaws builds more credibility than hiding them.
3. **Versus & Comparisons** — Customers compare options; be the unbiased guide. Create content like "Product A vs. Product B" even if you sell one of them.
4. **Reviews & Best in Class** — People search for "best [X]" constantly. Create honest reviews and best-of lists in your industry.
5. **Case Studies & Success Stories** — Real stories of real customers solving real problems. The closer the case study matches the prospect's situation, the more powerful it is.

### The CarMax Effect
CarMax disrupted used car sales by eliminating everything customers hated: negotiation, pressure, hidden pricing. They listened to what customers feared and removed those fears. The lesson: be the business that removes friction, not the one that adds it.

### The Ostrich Marketing Strategy (What NOT to Do)
Most businesses bury their heads in the sand and refuse to address:
- Pricing
- Competitor comparisons
- Their own weaknesses
- Bad reviews

This creates a trust vacuum that competitors fill.

### Assignment Selling
Use content to pre-sell prospects before the sales conversation:
- Assign specific articles or videos for prospects to read/watch before a sales call
- This pre-handles objections, educates the buyer, and dramatically increases close rates
- Salespeople become trusted advisors instead of pitchmen
- Saves 30–60 minutes per sales call

### Insourcing Content Creation
Don't outsource your content to an agency — the best content comes from inside the business:
- Your staff knows the questions customers ask
- Authenticity is higher when employees create content
- Hire a dedicated Content Manager (video, writing, SEO)
- The Content Manager is one of the best hires a business can make

### Content Never Sleeps
A piece of written content or video works 24/7 to educate and convert prospects, long after it's created. It's a permanent sales asset. Traditional ads stop the moment you stop paying; content compounds.

### The Revenue Attribution Model
Track which pieces of content contacts consumed before they became customers, and calculate the revenue attributed to that content. This proves ROI and guides future content investment.

## Actionable Takeaways
- Answer every question customers ask — start with pricing, it's the highest-traffic topic
- Write one article on your biggest pricing question this week; track the revenue it influences
- Run a "Big 5 audit" — do you have content addressing all 5 topics for your business?
- Practice assignment selling: give every prospect 2–3 pieces of content before your next sales call
- Hire an internal Content Manager rather than outsourcing to an agency
- Create comparison content ("Us vs. Them") — Google loves it and prospects need it
- Track which content pieces contributed to closed deals and double down on what works
- Your competitors' refusal to be transparent is your opportunity
- The teacher/trusted advisor framing beats the salesperson framing every time
- 70% of the buying decision is made before they contact you — your content is your sales team
